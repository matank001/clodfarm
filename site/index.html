clodfarm, say it out loud

Plant a mission tonight. Harvest the work in the morning.

A farm of Claude Code agents in one container. They split big jobs into sub-agents, check their work with your tests, and pace themselves on your account's real usage limits, so there's always room left for you.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/matank001/clodfarm/main/scripts/install.sh | sh

Needs Docker. It starts the container, then asks you to log in: open the link on any device, paste the code back.

A season on the farm

  1. Plant

    Give it a mission, queue tasks, or just talk to it from the Claude app.

    clodfarm mission "Build a CSV to Markdown CLI, with tests"

  2. Sprout

    Workers take tasks from the queue. Each one runs Claude Code in its own git worktree, so agents never step on each other.

  3. Split

    Big jobs fan out into sub-agents. When they finish, the parent picks up in its own conversation and merges their branches.

  4. Harvest

    Your check runs on the finished work. Only work that passes lands on your repo; a failing check sends the agent back to fix it.

Paced by the moon

Every agent run reports your account's actual 5-hour and weekly usage, straight from Claude Code. The farm reads those numbers, not guesses.

  • Agents stop at 80% of the week and 85% of any 5-hour window. The rest is yours.
  • The numbers include your own chats, so the farm backs off while you work.
  • Hit a limit and it sleeps until the reset, then carries on by itself.
  • It never draws on paid overage unless you allow it. With an API key, it keeps to a daily dollar cap instead.

What's in the barn

  • Steer it from your phone

    Remote Control stays connected, so the farm is a session in the Claude app and at claude.ai/code.

  • Nothing lands untested

    Set your test command once. Finished work is checked before it reaches your repo.

  • Survives restarts

    Crashed runs are retried, timed-out runs continue where they stopped, and a restarting box hands its tasks back at once.

  • Tells you when it matters

    Notifications to ntfy, Slack or Discord when a task fails, the farm pauses, or there's nothing left to do.

  • No dashboard to babysit

    The Claude app, one command line tool and the logs are the whole interface.

One box, or a whole farm

One box

One container on your machine or any server. Its state lives in a small file inside it, so there's no database to run. On AWS, one command deploys it with no open ports.

A whole farm

Point more boxes at one shared table, on your account or a teammate's own seat. They share one queue and one repo, and each account is paced on its own budget, so when one hits its limit the others keep working.

Questions

Is this allowed?

It drives the official Claude Code CLI, headless mode and Remote Control as documented. On a subscription it's meant for your own projects, on your own login. Anthropic's consumer terms forbid reselling or intermediating Claude usage: use Team or Enterprise seats for people working together, and an API key for commercial workloads. Read the Consumer Terms yourself; this isn't legal advice.

Will it lock me out of my own Claude?

That's what the pacing is for. By default the agents stop at 80% of your week and 85% of any 5-hour window, and the numbers include your own use.

What does it cost?

clodfarm is free and MIT-licensed. On a subscription you pay nothing beyond your plan. One box needs no database; on AWS you pay for the instance.

Is it safe to give agents a shell?

They run as an unprivileged user inside the container. Mount only what they may change, give git a key for one repo, and read the security notes: a prompt is not a security boundary.

Is clodfarm made by Anthropic?

No. It's an independent open-source project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic.
